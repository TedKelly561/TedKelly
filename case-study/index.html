Case study · field service operation

Running a warranty and repair operation on automation

A real field-service business, unnamed here to protect its customers and partners. The figures come from the owner's own records.

100–200tickets a month
30–40independent technicians
15–20vendor accounts sending work

The problem

Work arrives from warranty companies and vendors as emails, each in its own format. Every one had to be read, retyped into the ticket system, assigned to an independent technician, and tracked until the first call, the visit and the completion report were done. Each vendor sets its own first-contact deadline and wants its own status updates.

The work was spread across email, spreadsheets and a growing pile of Zapier automations. The Zapier bill kept creeping up, and when an automation failed, nobody found out until a vendor asked where their report was.

What was built

  • A ticket system in AppSheet, later rebuilt on the AppSheet API so other tools can create and update tickets automatically.
  • AI email intake: each work-order email is read by AI, turned into a ticket and checked field by field. Anything unclear goes to a person.
  • A morning digest: every day a script checks every open job and lists what needs attention, such as new work not yet logged, jobs with no technician, overdue first calls and reports not sent.
  • Vendor update tracking: a daily feed of reports that haven't gone out yet, before the vendor has to chase them.
  • Zapier work moved to scripts: the busiest Zapier automations were rebuilt as scripts that cost nothing per run.

How it was switched on safely

Every change followed the safety method: shadow mode first, one install a day, every install checked against an export, a rollback copy of every version, an independent AI review, and anything unclear handed to a person. The business kept running normally the whole time.

What it costs to run

At 100–200 emails a month and about a cent each, the AI reading costs roughly $1–2 a month. The scripts run on the business's existing Google Workspace at no extra cost. Zapier is kept only for the jobs it does best, on a smaller plan.
